Nine chapters for the person who runs the instance: how to deploy it, every variable it reads, who may log in and what they may do, what expires on its own, what it sends out, how to back it up and watch it, and what every error message means. They assume Concepts.

  • Chapter 1

    Deployment

    The image, Docker and Compose, Kustomize, the Helm chart, SQLite or PostgreSQL, scaling, upgrades, verifying a release.

  • Chapter 2

    Configuration

    Every environment variable with its default, root users, caches, CA certificates, the settings in the database.

  • Chapter 3

    Authentication

    The modes, local accounts and approval, LDAP and Active Directory, OpenID Connect, developer mode.

  • Chapter 4

    Roles and permissions

    The ten permissions, the five roles, maintainers, groups from the directory, token scopes.

  • Chapter 5

    Administration

    Producers, versions and Consumers, sanshain-branches, retention and cleanup, settings, the audit log, the Danger Zone.

  • Chapter 6

    Webhooks

    Hooks in three scopes, the events and their bodies, the signature, the allow-list, the outbox and its retries.

  • Chapter 7

    Backup and restore

    The SQLite snapshot and its CronJob, pg_dump, retention, restoring either engine.

  • Chapter 8

    Observability

    Every metric by name, traces over OTLP, the log filters, the observability page, the probes.

  • Chapter 9

    Troubleshooting

    Every message the service answers or logs, quoted, with cause and fix.

The complete administrative surface with the permission of every route is the generated Maintenance API; the architecture is in the Reference. The operator walkthrough with a fresh 2.6.0 screenshot set follows in #57.

Checked against v2.6.0.
